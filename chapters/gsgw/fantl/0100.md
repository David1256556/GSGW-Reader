# Chapter 99 - [Dokkaebi Workshop], II

I knew.

I knew that the Disaster Management Bureau was rigorously managing the information about this ghost story, ‘Dokkaebi Workshop’, to prevent any leaks.

But that’s precisely... where my knowledge stopped!

‘I had no idea how the Bureau was *managing* the ghost story....’

After all, not even a mention of this existed in the \<Darkness Exploration Records\>!

Who would have guessed they’d go as far as conducting undercover surveillance within the ghost story itself, screening visitors and monitoring the equipment production?

A Disaster Management Bureau agent, a human pretending to be a dokkaebi!

[Oh my, why are you taking so long to answer?]
p$

My head was spinning.

‘......What do I do now?’

Wait a second, what have I been saying up until now?

With my blood running cold, I mentally replayed our earlier conversation, which flashed through my mind like a panorama.

Thankfully, no critical mistakes... none at all.

‘...I’m fine!’

I could still recover. I could salvage this....

With that in mind, I deliberately displayed a slightly pale, nervous expression.

“I’m sorry, Elder, but I wasn’t given permission to discuss such matters....”

[.......]

I know nothing.

I am but a clueless rookie who hadn’t caught on that this ‘dokkaebi’ was actually a senior agent.

A dokkaebi from a ghost story interrogating me about my identity had caught me off guard, leaving me fumbling to respond as best I could!

‘Please let this work!’

I subtly raised my head, wearing a hesitant expression, as if thinking, *What do I do about this dokkaebi?*

‘Made it.’

Was he leaning the way I wanted?

$p

p$

It seemed like the agent intended to keep up the dokkaebi charade.

‘...The deflection worked.’

A classic case of teasing the junior.

I’d encouraged his desire to leave this rookie utterly dumbfounded someday, when a senior clued me in: *‘That guy was an agent!’*

I’d guessed as much after seeing him sniff the ginseng stored in my tattoo, just like a real dokkaebi.

‘Had mimicking a dokkaebi made his abilities and personality similar to theirs?’

Maybe he’d used some kind of ‘equipment’ that did that.

After all, dokkaebi couldn’t resist pranks like that.

“Th-thank you.”

I gave a deep bow, playing along.

$p

[Come now, tell me what dokkaebi item you’d like. You could tell me in song or even dance....]
p$

Now he was trying to reward the rookie.

Perhaps he figured the wild ginseng I’d given him was a valuable item I’d gotten somewhere, then handed over too readily in a fluster.

‘...The most natural response would be,’

“I-It’s fine, Elder! Then... could you extend your goodwill to the next, umm, ‘government Kim-seobangs’ who visit? As generously as you feel today.”

$p
[...Oho, really?]

“Yes!”

$p
[Alright then, Kim-seobang. I always make sure to earn my liquor!]
p$

I smiled brightly and nodded, and the dokkaebi seemed satisfied, even doing a little dance.

Good. That topic seemed to have ended well.

The conversation then naturally shifted to the popsocket I’d brought with me.

$p
[So, the reason you’re here today, Kim-seobang, is... to get this trinket repaired, yes?]
p$

“...Yes.”

Here came the real problem.

This agent had already recognized that my popsocket wasn’t made here....

Cold sweat dripped from my palms.

Sure enough, the dokkaebi-disguised agent nonchalantly probed further.

$p
[Where did this item come from?]
p$

“Uh, I’m not sure. I received it as a government-issued gift....”

$p

[Risky, hm.]
p$

“.......”

$p
[You never know when a daytime dokkaebi might appear!]
p$

True, the Disaster Management Bureau wasn’t relying solely on this workshop to produce equipment for agents anymore.

‘My Silver Heart badge came from somewhere else, too.’

Fortunately, the agent seemed to misinterpret the popsocket as something from another similar workshop.

A convenient misunderstanding.

‘Lucky.’

Seeing the agent mention a daytime dokkaebi at the end while staying in character as a dokkaebi helped me relax a little.

It allowed me to wryly admit one thing.

‘Still, I’m screwed.’

This agent would undoubtedly remember me, the random visitor who showed up today and handed over ginseng.

‘As soon as he leaves this ghost story and talks with other Disaster Management Bureau agents, I’ll be tracked as a person of interest.’

...Was there really no way out of this?

$p
[Anyway, I’ll fix up this trinket for you, so sit tight!]
p$

“Yes....”

The agent, imitating a dokkaebi, disappeared into the tiled house, the ‘workshop’, with my popsocket in hand.

He was probably handing it off to the other dokkaebi inside.

I waited quietly.

A dark thought crossed my mind.

What if the agent pieced everything together, confiscated my popsocket, and placed an emergency call to the Disaster Management Bureau? That thought alone made cold sweat trickle down my back.

Thankfully, it didn’t take long.

$p
[Here, take your trinket!]
p$

The returning agent tossed the popsocket lightly toward me. I barely restrained myself from cursing as I carefully caught it.

The crack on the popsocket remained.

However, the fissure was now filled with something smooth and glossy.

An adhesive that shimmered jade-green.

“Ah, is it fully repaired now?”

$p
[Ohoh, even rice needs time to simmer. You think an elder’s craft can be completed as easily as slurping cold porridge? Wait for the adhesive to dry!]

[Don’t wear it for a month. Handle it like a swaddled baby, as precious as gold or jade. While doing so, wish for what form you’d like it to take. Then, it’ll transform into a splendid trinket!]
p$

A month?

“...Thank you.”

I carefully held the popsocket.

‘...Feels like the crack would open right up if I put any pressure on it.’

In other words, I’d have to handle it delicately for an entire month while the adhesive sets.

Which meant....

‘I won’t be able to access the wiki for a month.’

I’d been re-reading it daily, trying to commit as much as possible to memory as clearly as I could. Now I couldn’t keep up that routine.

Human memory has its limits, after all. I felt uneasy... but there was no alternative. I had to endure. Getting a new popsocket seemed out of reach.

‘At least I managed to get it repaired.’

That left one problem.

How do I leave this place naturally?

$p
[Now now, young Kim-seobang, how about a bite of that buckwheat cake you brought... Let’s chat about what government Kim-seobangs have been up to lately!]
p$

But dear Elder, I have no idea either....

‘If this conversation drags on, I’ll be exposed in no time.’

I might not just end up on a wanted list. I could be tied up and hauled off to the Disaster Management Bureau’s glass cell right now.

...Fine.

I steeled my resolve.

‘I’d be caught eventually, anyway.’

Then... even if it makes me a little more memorable, I’m going for it!

“Yes, Elder. I, uh....”

*Cough—*

Suddenly, I coughed up blood without holding back.

With a gurgle, clotted blood spilled down my chin. I hurriedly wiped it away with my sleeve, feigning a panicked expression as I looked at the dokkaebi.

“I-I’m sorry....”

It wasn’t just to stir alarm.

The next two days were spent in genuine recovery.

Not being able to use the popsocket made me anxious, so I tried jotting down what I remembered from the wiki. But I stopped when I realized I might be unintentionally distorting or creating details that were never true.

Instead, I obsessively replayed the information in my mind, which made time pass more quickly.

And then....

<br>
“Kim Soleum-ssi.”

“Section Chief.”

Three days later, back at work, I met Section Chief Lee Jaheon again.

“How are you feeling?”

“I’m fine. You should have an additional consultation by the end of this month, Kim Soleum-ssi.”

“......Yes.”

Fortunately, he seemed fine, too.

Section Chief Lee Jaheon didn’t ask me anything further.

Neither about Braun’s abilities, such as ‘Lights Out’, nor about the mascot made of a mass of black water that had chased me.

Even when I shared my side of the story as much as I could, he only responded with a calm, “*I see.*”

‘Consistent as always.’

Because of that, I also found it hard to ask him any further questions....

Besides, after three days of sick leave, I had too much work piled up to waste time prying.

‘Wow, how am I supposed to finish all this?’

After yet another irregular escape, the entire D-Squad, except for the two entry-level employees who’d come to support us, took immediate leave.

The pressure from all sides was relentless.

I spent the day writing reports with the Section Chief, doing interviews, and even calling Director Cheong to express my gratitude.

In the midst of it all, I had to include in my report how I’d used the taxi Darkness to escape Death Lane, which left me racking my brain over how to explain how I knew to summon it.

‘Should I just say I saw some flyer advertisement in Death Lane?’

But wouldn’t that count as contaminating the manual?

My head was spinning.

“The taxi you summoned is categorized as a pending Darkness under manual review.”

“...!”

“That means it’s information that can’t be obtained through normal use of the company’s systems.”

Whoa.

As I turned to look at Section Chief Lee Jaheon with a chill creeping over me, the lizard told me.

“So, I changed the recorded caller.”

“......Who is it now?”

“Me.”

Wait.

“You changed the report to say *you* were the one who called the taxi, Squad Leader?”

“Yes.”

I was starting to grasp his modus operandi.

“...Was this both a reward for my significant role in escaping Death Lane and you taking responsibility for our joint operation as the squad’s leader?”

“Yes.”

And that wasn’t the end of it.

“Oh, by the way, do you recall, Squad Leader? You once promised me some equipment during the pantry ghost story....”

“Yes. It’ll be ready by next week.”

Wow.

So what if he’s a bit frustrating? He’s the dream boss!

‘You’ll just have to put up with the frustration yourself!’

*Got it, Kim Soleum?!*

New personal equipment in the works, a squad with a new status ahead.

A new chapter in my corporate life was on the horizon.
