# Chapter 337 - Sekwang Special City Disaster, III

Why is Agent Bronze... wearing a Sekwang High uniform and looking so young?

“......”

The sheer absurdity of the situation nearly left me stunned, but I forced myself to collect my thoughts and assess things immediately.

Agent Bronze looked even slightly younger than his appearance as a high schooler in the ghost story ‘Within the Shadow of Darkness.’

And it wasn’t just the uniform that had changed.

His expression and overall presence were unmistakably youthful.

“Uh...”

And he didn’t recognize me.

‘...Has he been assimilated into the Sekwang Special City ghost story?’

Like me, placed here as a citizen of Sekwang Special City on May 4th.

...Wait.

If everyone else who fell here is in this same state, then how am I supposed to get them out?

How do I convince them?

The relief of finding a companion was brief, and I was immediately faced with another urgent dilemma.

I desperately turned my thoughts over and over. While I did, the high schooler Ryu Jaekwan, who had been running behind me, caught his breath and chose his words.

“About the bicycle earlier... are you okay?”

“I’m fine.”

I looked at his almost boyish face.

There was confusion and concern mixed in, but considering he’d just witnessed something straight out of a ghost story becoming reality, he was remarkably calm.

‘...He said the first time Agent Bronze got caught up in a ghost story and was just rescued by the Disaster Management Bureau was when he was a student.’

Could he have been reflected here from an even earlier point than that? No, what’s important right now is how to get him to come with me.

I pretended to glance over his uniform, as if checking for a name tag.

“More importantly... are you a student?”

“Yes.”

“Is your destination Sekwang Technical High School, by any chance? That looks like their uniform.”

“Yes, it is, but...”

“That works out. I’m heading there as well.”

I moved toward the shared bike rack and began smashing the locks with a rock.

“...!”

“It’ll be difficult to rent a public bike with a smartphone now. This will be faster than trying.”

“......”

“Did you see people swelling up on the way here? Or disaster alerts?”

“...Yes.”

“From now on, listen only to the alert sound. Don’t read the messages. And if you see people walking together along the roadside, never approach them.”

One bike’s lock finally broke, but I didn’t stop—I smashed the next one too.

Then I pulled a bike free and held it out to him.

“Can you ride?”

Ryu Jaekwan stared at me with trembling eyes, then nodded firmly.

***

I rode in front, leading the way.

Behind me, high schooler Ryu Jaekwan followed, riding the shared bike I’d freed.

Judging by how tightly he clenched his teeth and kept up, it seemed Agent Bronze had shown promise in bike riding from the start...

Skillfully avoiding the sounds of the disaster alerts and screams echoing from all directions, I kept detouring toward Sekwang Technical High School.

But those warning signs would disappear soon enough.

...Once there were no survivors left nearby, the alerts would naturally stop.

‘We have to move fast.’

My mind raced as I planned our route, and then I spotted something.

A convenience store.

“Wait a moment.”

“Yes?”

Making sure Ryu Jaekwan’s movements weren’t wobbly, I stopped for a moment in front of the convenience store. Then I quickly opened the door, went inside, and swept items into my arms.

‘Food first.’

I didn’t take too much—mobility was essential.

Instead, after some thought, I grabbed two sturdy long umbrellas and a toolkit.

While paying, I spoke to the part-time clerk at the counter, who’d been glancing at a book with earbuds in.

“Get out of here quickly.”

“Pardon?”

“There was a biochemical terror attack near City Hall, and it’s spreading. Run to the subway station as fast as you can.”

“!!”

Only then did the clerk check their phone—and immediately start frantically scrolling with a panicked face.

As I left, I glimpsed the clerk hurriedly gathering supplies, just like I had...

Outside, I turned to Ryu Jaekwan, who was standing awkwardly beside his bike.

“Open your bag.”

“Pardon?”

I put two small bottles of water and a calorie bar into his school bag, then handed him an umbrella and strapped it to the bike.

“We don’t know how things will go. It’s better to be prepared. Use the umbrella to shove things away if you have to.”

“Th-thank you.”

He gripped his bag, hesitating.

“I’ll pay you back.”

“This isn’t the time to take money from a student. Get on the bike. We’re heading for Sekwang High, fast.”

“But... yes.”

He looked full of questions, but wiped his sweat and grabbed the bike again, clearly understanding the seriousness of the situation.

Still, he eventually asked,

“How do you... know all this so well?”

“...I’m used to it.”

“Pardon?”

I changed the subject.

“More importantly, school hours have passed. Why are you heading to school now?”

“I had to stop by the bank, so I’m late.”

“Did you contact your family?”

“...It’s fine.”

That careful answer told me his assigned backstory here was likely close to reality.
