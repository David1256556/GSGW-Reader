# Chapter 343 - Sekwang Special City Disaster, IX

“......”

In the first place.

Why did I keep exploring this dangerous Sekwang Special City?

It was because of a vague sense of expectation.

The expectation that, if I dug into this enormous, secret-ridden ghost story, I might somehow reach the fundamental truth of this world.

And then...

An expectation that I might learn why this was happening to me.

That I might discover the cause or reason behind being suddenly thrown into the world of Darkness Exploration Records and forced to suffer through incomprehensible hardship...

I had just realized that those expectations had been rewarded.

In a direction I had never anticipated.

‘So this place was trying to invite me...’
p$

The Cheerful Research Institute had tried to summon me, a person from a “safe world without ghost stories,” simply to prove that such a world existed...

And because of that, the disaster in Sekwang Special City occurred.

My heart went cold.

‘Then... do the hundreds of thousands who died in Sekwang Special City, and the city being sealed off, ultimately have something to do with me...?’

...No! Don’t get confused.

‘Did I ask them to summon me?’

Absolutely not.

Like the other citizens of Sekwang City, I was dragged into this. Caught up in the insane experiments of the institutions of this damned ghost story world...

I was dragged in, and it just happened to be me.

“......”

In an instant, something like searing pain surged from my chest up to my head.

It was anger.

Barely managing to exhale softly, I looked at the researcher from the Cheerful Research Institute.

My words came out strangely dry.

In a tone befitting an agent.

“So in the end, you’re saying you tried to summon a person from a world without supernatural phenomena, a completely absurd notion. And... you failed, didn’t you?”

“......”

“An entire metropolis turned into a ghost story. Why did you attempt something like this without even having the ability to take responsibility? What confidence did you have?”

“We didn’t do this. It wasn’t supposed to fail. This attempt—”

“‘Wasn’t supposed to fail’? What kind of irresponsible statement is that? If you’re a research institute, it’s only natural to prepare for the possibility of failure.”

Researcher Lee Heoun’s face grew even paler.

Fine, then.

“How are you going to clean this up? Where are the other researchers right now?”

“They evacuated due to the fire. And there was a contingency plan. But it’s not working now. We were deceived too. We—”

“What kind of irresponsible nonsense is that?”

“That’s exactly what I think.”

...!

I snapped my head around.

The person I had forgotten was quietly speaking.

The Counselor, his expression gone.

“I think so too. This is unbelievably irresponsible.”

“......”

“I was very clearly promised something by them.”

The air twisted strangely.

“That on the day their efforts succeeded, this city would become a place safer for people than anywhere else on the surface.”

Lee Heoun gave no answer.

No—he couldn’t.

I saw him clutch at his own throat.

‘...!’

In that fantastical space filled with colorful toys, he rolled on the floor with choking sounds.

Yet the Counselor smiled at me and explained calmly, as if kindly going over pre-session instructions.

“I have lived in this region, once called Gwangjin and now Sekwang Special City, for a very long time. I used to harm people here and there, but after growing attached to them and gaining wisdom, I have watched over and protected them ever since. That is why those words pleased me.”

His previously composed, rational tone grew softer and gentler.

As if he were returning to his true form.

“Because the officials who followed these people also gave me their word, I offered them the ground beneath this land and listened to their requests. Then, one day, I received a particularly earnest plea.”

“...What kind of request?”

“They asked to borrow my mysterious bead for a short while.”

At the Counselor’s, or rather the spirit’s, gesture, a bright, perfectly round sphere floated in midair above his finger.

It glowed with dazzling, multicolored brilliance tinged with red.

I remembered that Assistant Manager Eun Haje had once used a similar bead to infiltrate the Myeongchanlan Sect disguised as an old man...

But it had been only the size of a finger and had not seemed nearly as powerful.

“Have you ever heard the folk tales about the Fox Bead? Not a fruit, but a bead possessed by a fox spirit.”

It sparkled.

“There is a theory that it originated from a dragon’s Yeouiju[1]{Editor’s Note: The Yeouiju (여의주) is a mythical Korean dragon pearl that grants wish-fulfillment and omnipotence. Its primary importance in folklore is acting as the catalyst for transformation, as lesser serpents (Imugis) must possess it to become true dragons and ascend to heaven.}, but that is probably because a similar story is told about it... that it grants wishes.”

“...!”

Soon, the fantastical image vanished.

But I realized that it resembled the egg-like object floating at the center of the Dream Incubator’s tank...

‘...A wish.’

Don’t tell me.

“Was that thing you lent them used in this experiment?”

“That seems likely, doesn’t it?”

A laugh followed.

“Without knowing that, I hurried to retrieve my bead and take action. Guarding my territory and repelling other impurities was something the Disaster Management Bureau had repeatedly asked of me through careful rituals as well. But...”

His voice hardened.

“He says the bead is gone.”

“......”

“There is no way now. No way to protect this city from the evil infiltration outside, from the infection spreading like a plague.”

The researcher’s face turned blue. His hands trembled as though he might lose consciousness at any moment.

‘...There’s no point in letting Lee Heoun die like this.’

This wasn’t solely his responsibility, and we would lose the information.

I finally spoke quickly.

“Can’t you at least try to trace how the bead disappeared?”

“......”

“And if that doesn’t work, we should see whether there’s another way to save the city. ...By interrogating the researcher.”

......

......

“That’s true.”

“Ghk!”

Lee Heoun jerked his hands away from his throat as if shocked by electricity.

After gasping on the floor a few times, he spoke with eyes still focused.

“I—I’m sorry... I’m sorry...”

“Your apology is unnecessary. First, we need to find a way to deal with this. Explain how the experiment proceeded, from the beginning.”

“Yes, cough, so we...”

But at that moment—

Researcher Lee Heoun’s right ear fell to the floor with a soft thud.

“...!”

“Ah.”

The researcher groped around and picked up the ear.

What had fallen was now unmistakably a plastic toy...

“......”

I turned my head toward the corridor.

Beyond Lee Jaheon and the others, the gaudy hallway looked strangely more static than before.

Some of the colorful toys had stopped working, standing still or losing their vitality...

They looked like things that had lost their mystery.

“...Please escape quickly. Th-this office won’t hold much longer either. You need to get out...”

Lee Heoun spoke frantically.

“We’re currently protected from the fire and the disaster outside, but the flames will reach here soon. There isn’t much time left!”

Wait.

“Are you saying that what’s preventing the fire from spreading to this floor... is disappearing?”

“Yes...”

I recalled how the Counselor’s hand, when placed into the monster, had turned mushy before vanishing as if it had dissolved.

...Then.

Could it be...?

“Is Sekwang Special City right now... losing supernatural phenomena altogether?”

“......”

A faint smile appeared on the researcher’s face.

“That’s correct.”

...!!

“So strictly speaking, we did achieve the objective of our experiment. The invitation must have succeeded...!”

A faint thrill could be heard in the researcher’s voice.

“Someone... has arrived.”

My head went cold with emotions I couldn’t tell apart, anger or horror.

But my sense of reason caught on to the fact that something was not right.

“That’s strange. If supernatural phenomena are disappearing, shouldn’t things be getting safer? Why is the outside in that state?”

“That’s why this isn’t our intention. It’s a result that’s impossible under our goals...”

“Impossible?”

“Yes. Today, all we intended to do was establish and prove the existence of a ‘place without ghost stories’...”

His tired voice sank lower.

“Someone interfered and subtly altered the ritual.”

“......”

“And they must have set the fire to prevent us from responding. That would be...”

“Usually, the one who benefits the most is the culprit.”

Everyone’s gaze turned toward the door.

Reporter Eun Haje was scratching her head.

“Honestly, half of what you’re saying sounds like nonsense to me, but the overall context is right in my field. That’s how the world usually works. So, who benefits the most from this mess?”

The researcher’s eyes sank.

It was a valid point, but given his nature, he seemed unlikely to arrive at that conclusion himself.

Then.

‘Let me make it more specific.’

“Good point, citizen.”

I added casually,

“It seems that you have a few people who come to mind. Among them, was there anyone who suddenly disappeared from the scene right after the experiment?”

“......!”

The dazed researcher turned toward me, then spoke in shock.

“There is.”

“Iyaa.”

I heard Reporter Eun Haje’s uneasy voice.

“Can you tell us their name or appearance?”

The researcher staggered up and rummaged through what looked like his desk.

From the bottom drawer, he pulled out a teal hardbound book labeled Cheerful Research Institute Founding Commemorative Publication, flipping through it rapidly.

A group photo labeled “Corporate Cooperation.”

The researcher pointed at one person.

“It’s this person.”

“......”

A numbing shock spread through my head.

“She’s an entity from a phenomenon classified as dragon-type. She was definitely at the scene on City Hall’s rooftop until the experiment was underway today, but at some point, she vanished without a word...”

“Hmm...”

Eun Haje spoke uneasily.

“This feels like a very obvious situation. Didn’t the partner company stab you in the back and run off with the research results? Or did they manipulate the conditions to get the outcome they wanted, only for it to blow up in their faces?”

“...!”

Shock and rejection crossed Lee Heoun’s face, then shifted into resignation and bitterness.

“...That may be the case. We’re already...”

The next moment, Lee Heoun’s thumb fell to the floor.

It too was plastic.

“...!”

“There really isn’t much time left now.”

Urgency filled his face.

“Please escape to the subway. This experiment’s range was limited to the ‘surface,’ so the subway, which lies outside the origin point, should be relatively safe. You people should at least be safe...”

Then he bowed deeply toward the Counselor.

“I’m sorry. Given the circumstances, there’s a high probability that your treasure was used in this experiment.”

“......”

“So it might still be on the rooftop... but I can’t be certain. And there are no mysterious toys left in this institute with enough power to reach there.”

The Counselor didn’t reply.

Lee Heoun couldn’t raise his head, and the movements of the corridor and office’s flashy toys began stopping intermittently.

Then Lee Heoun’s foot fell off with a dull sound.

A toy shoe like that of a nutcracker soldier rolled across the floor. The researcher staggered.

High school student Ryu Jaekwan, who had been near the door, approached cautiously.

“I—I can help support you...”

“Thank you, but...”

A bitter smile spread across Lee Heoun’s face.

“I can only live inside this institute. I’ve already lost most of my body.”

“......”

“But that researcher... he’ll be able to live even if he leaves.”

He pointed at Researcher Lee Jaheon near the door.

“He’s from another department. He barely participated in this research. He bears no responsibility, so please take him with you if possible...”

Despite those thoughtful words, Lee Jaheon simply stared at him, seemingly unmoved.

“You should evacuate too... don’t rely on that strange button item anymore. Leave the office and escape now. Go.”

...Button item?

I couldn’t see Lee Jaheon’s face, but I did see him step into the corridor instead of nodding.

“I’ll guide you to the subway entrance.”

That seemed to reassure Lee Heoun.

“I’ll look for another method, so it’s best if everyone leaves.”

“Hyuu.”

Starting with Reporter Eun Haje, everyone exited the office.

And when even the Counselor stepped into the corridor—

“......”

“Agent?”

I quietly stood in front of Lee Heoun.

“Researcher. Are you almost certain that a person from a ‘world without ghost stories’ has come here?”

“That’s... yes.”

“Does that outcome make you happy?”

Beyond regret and despair, a flicker of achievement as a researcher passed briefly across Lee Heoun’s face.

“For that alone, yes.”

“I see.”

I lowered my head and whispered into his ear.

“Then does it not matter what happens to the being you summoned?”

“...What?”

“What you summoned wasn’t a spirit, but a human. Just a human.”

My voice spoke slowly, deliberately.

“Have you ever imagined what would happen to an ordinary person, someone who had lived a normal life without ever encountering supernatural phenomena, if they were suddenly dragged into an unfamiliar environment like this?”

“What are you—”

Lee Heoun’s puzzled gaze changed the moment our eyes met.

“......”

“W-wait.”

His eyes trembled.

“Could it be...”

I silently looked down at him.

His eyes hurriedly scanned my appearance.

They stopped at my clothes, and his pupils shook.

As if he had realized they weren’t an official agent’s uniform, but the attire of a cooperative spirit, the Counselor.

“...Agent, what is your name?”

“Who knows.”

“Are you... a citizen of Sekwang Special City?”

“Who knows.”

Lee Heoun looked me over desperately, as if trying to find a clue, or something to remember.

Instead of wavering, I leaned closer and whispered.

“But there’s one thing I want to tell you.”

“Yes—yes! What is it...”

“In my opinion, even if your invitation had succeeded... the one you summoned would never cooperate with you.”

“......”

“Never.”

Lee Heoun froze.
